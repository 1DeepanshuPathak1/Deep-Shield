About the project

Deep Shield is a major project built at Sir M. Visvesvaraya Institute of Technology. It detects manipulated media and, just as importantly, explains why it reached each verdict.

Audio detection

A clip is converted into 17 numbers: 13 Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients that describe the shape of the vocal tract, plus spectral centroid, chroma, zero-crossing rate and RMSE energy. A Random Forest of 100 decision trees classifies that fingerprint. Synthetic speech tends to be too smooth and too regular in exactly these measurements.

Video detection

Twenty frames are sampled evenly across the clip. MTCNN locates faces, each face is resized to 100×100, and a ResNet50 + EfficientNetB0 ensemble scores it. The two backbones look for different artefacts — blending seams at the jaw and hairline, and texture that is too uniform across the skin.

The three checks, and what each measures

A swapped face and a fully generated scene are different problems. A face-swap detector looks for the seam where one face was pasted onto another — but a generated scene has no seam, because nothing was pasted. It is synthetic all the way through. That is why the second check exists.

CheckMethodCatches
Face swapping MTCNN face detection, then a ResNet50 + EfficientNetB0 ensemble on each crop A real video with someone else's face pasted in
AI generation 22 forensic measurements fused by a gradient-boosted classifier, alongside a pretrained diffusion classifier Footage generated outright, with or without a face
Voice 17 acoustic features voted on by 100 decision trees Cloned or synthesised speech
What the generation check actually measures. A camera and a generative model leave different fingerprints in the pixels. Real footage carries sensor noise, lens blur that varies across the frame, and a frequency spectrum that falls away irregularly. Generated footage tends to be too clean and too even: sharper edges than optics produce, an unnaturally smooth noise floor, high-frequency detail that is over-represented because the image was upsampled, and sharpness that is suspiciously uniform across the whole frame rather than varying with depth. The check also tests the distribution of DCT coefficients against Benford's law, which real photographs follow and generated images often do not.

What do train, dev and eval mean?

The audio dataset arrives already divided into three groups. The split exists to stop a model from simply memorising its answers, and each group has a strict job.

SplitJobDoes the model learn from it?Files
train Teaching set. The model studies these clips and adjusts itself to fit them. Yes — this is the only data it learns from 13,185
dev Practice exam, also called validation. Used while building the model to tune settings and spot overfitting early. No, but it does influence the choices made 12,843
eval Final exam. Held back untouched until the very end, so the score is an honest estimate on unseen data. No, and it is never looked at during training 32,746
Why the separation matters. A student who has seen the exam paper in advance will score well without understanding the subject. A model tested on its own training data does exactly the same thing. Keeping eval sealed off is what makes the reported accuracy trustworthy. If a model scores highly on train but poorly on dev, it has memorised rather than learned — that gap is called overfitting, and you can see it happening in the loss curve on the Results page.

Honest limitations

What this system does not do, stated plainly.

It needs a visible face

The video model scores faces, not scenes. A clip with no detectable face cannot be judged.

Accuracy is dataset-bound

The quoted figures are measured on held-out data from the same sources. Media from a different generator may score lower.

It is not evidence

Deep Shield is a research project and a screening aid. It is not forensic proof and should not be treated as such.
